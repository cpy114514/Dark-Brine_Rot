# Solid ship fragment meshes — 2026-10-07

All initial ship fragments and secondary fragments now have non-trigger MeshColliders and a dynamic Rigidbody. Previously only 20 playable deck sections were solid: other fragments moved by directly setting their transforms and most had no collider; certain hull sections used trigger colliders only. That allowed the visual wreck pieces to pass through one another regardless of their apparent shape.

`Story1WreckMeshCollision` builds colliders from the original visible mesh. The 20 decks and 24 hulls use their 3–5 authored child mesh pieces as a compound collider, in the original relative positions and scales. This preserves recesses better than a single convex envelope. Smaller original details and secondary splinters use their own mesh as a convex collider. No generic rectangular collision box is substituted. Convex cooking still envelopes concavity within an individual child/detail mesh: this is not a claim of exact arbitrary concave triangle collision on a dynamic Rigidbody. Unity requires convex pieces for dynamic mesh bodies; see [Unity mesh collider guidance](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/mesh-colliders-introduction.html).

`Story1WreckDebris` now shares `Story1WreckFloatBody` gravity and softened water forces with the boards. ContinuousSpeculative collision detection, 10 position solver iterations and 4 velocity iterations apply per fragment. Contact offset is 8 mm. Buoyant wreck pieces remain active; non-buoyant pieces retire after sinking 10 m below the local surface. Animated Sahur hurtboxes ignore fragment collisions to avoid pinning wood; the CharacterController continues to handle locomotion contacts. Shark anatomical solving, attack contact and boarding queries inspect every compound mesh part.
